HOSPITAL COURSE

Ms. Echevarría is a 99-year-old Spanish-speaking female with extensive past medical history as above who presented to the Emergency Department on September 14, 2019, following a witnessed mechanical fall at her assisted living facility. The patient tripped over a rug and fell forward onto her hands and knees. There was no loss of consciousness, no head strike, and no seizure activity witnessed. The patient complained of bilateral knee pain and mild confusion.

In the Emergency Department, she was found to be alert but mildly confused compared to her baseline. Vital signs were stable with blood pressure 142/78 mmHg, heart rate 88 bpm (irregularly irregular), respiratory rate 18, oxygen saturation 96% on room air, and temperature 100.8°F. Physical examination revealed tenderness over bilateral knees without obvious deformity, ecchymosis, or effusion. No focal neurologic deficits were identified. Cardiovascular examination was consistent with her known atrial fibrillation.

Radiographic imaging of bilateral knees and pelvis showed no acute fracture or dislocation, with findings consistent with moderate degenerative joint disease. CT head without contrast demonstrated age-appropriate cerebral atrophy and chronic small vessel ischemic changes, but no acute intracranial hemorrhage or mass effect. Urinalysis was notable for pyuria, bacteriuria, and positive nitrites. Urine culture subsequently grew >100,000 CFU/mL Escherichia coli sensitive to ceftriaxone, ciprofloxacin, and trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole.

Laboratory studies revealed: White blood cell count 11,200/μL with left shift, hemoglobin 10.2 g/dL (baseline 10-11), creatinine 1.1 mg/dL (baseline), INR 2.3 (therapeutic for atrial fibrillation), glucose 178 mg/dL. Liver function tests were within normal limits.

The patient was admitted to the internal medicine service for observation and treatment of UTI. Intravenous ceftriaxone 1 gram daily was initiated. Her acute confusion improved significantly within 24 hours of antibiotic therapy and hydration. Pain management was achieved with acetaminophen 650 mg every 6 hours as needed; NSAIDs were avoided given her renal disease and warfarin therapy. Physical therapy evaluated the patient and recommended continued assistance with ambulation and return to baseline functional status with walker use.

The patient's home medications were continued throughout hospitalization with careful monitoring of INR given antibiotic interaction with warfarin. Her atrial fibrillation remained rate-controlled on her home regimen of verapamil and digoxin. Blood glucose control was maintained with home insulin regimen.

By hospital day 2 (September 16), the patient had returned to her baseline mental status per family report, was afebrile for >24 hours, ambulatory with walker and assistance, and tolerating oral intake well. She was deemed appropriate for discharge back to her assisted living facility with close outpatient follow-up.

GOALS OF CARE DISCUSSION

An extensive goals-of-care discussion was held on September 15, 2019, with the patient's family, including her daughter Maria Echevarría (healthcare power of attorney) and son Roberto Echevarría. The discussion was conducted with the assistance of professional Spanish interpretation services to ensure the patient's participation to the extent possible given her moderate Alzheimer's disease.

The family confirmed that the patient has established DNR/DNI (Do Not Resuscitate/Do Not Intubate) status, documented in her advance directive dated March 2019. The family expressed understanding that at age 99 with multiple comorbidities including metastatic colorectal cancer, the focus of care is on comfort, quality of life, and symptomatic management. The patient is currently receiving palliative chemotherapy (FOLFOX regimen) through her oncologist, Dr. Patricia Silverman, with the goal of disease control and maintaining functional status rather than cure.

The family wishes to continue the current treatment plan including chemotherapy as long as the patient tolerates it and maintains acceptable quality of life. They understand that should the patient experience clinical deterioration, significant decline, or intolerable side effects, the focus would shift entirely to comfort measures. Palliative care consultation has been offered and the family is considering this for future coordination.

All parties agreed that hospitalization should be avoided when possible, with management of acute issues at the assisted living facility when feasible. The patient should not undergo cardiopulmonary resuscitation, mechanical ventilation, or intensive care unit admission. Natural death is anticipated and accepted by patient and family. This discussion was documented in the electronic medical record and communicated to the patient's outpatient providers and assisted living facility nursing staff.
