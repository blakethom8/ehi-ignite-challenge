§2Critical issues — fix before submission

1. Localhost URL is leaking into every page footer Critical

Every page shows http://127.0.0.1:8899/report/drafts/ehi-atlas-phase1-submission.html and a timestamp 5/8/26, 8:25 PM in the footer. This was generated by printing an HTML page from a browser without disabling browser-injected headers/footers. To a judge this reads as "rendered from a dev server, not a finalized PDF." It's the single most fixable polish issue.

Fix: regenerate the PDF either via Chrome's print dialog with "Headers and footers" UNCHECKED, or via weasyprint / Puppeteer / Playwright with explicit margin and header config. Do this no matter what else changes. 5 minutes of work, immediate quality bump.

2. The "wireframes" section is not actually wireframes Critical

The official rules require "screen views, workflows, UX/UI assets, mockups of how users interact with the proposed solution." Page 4 ("Product Workflow") is text descriptions inside boxes — Source Intake, Harmonized Record, Patient Context, Publish Chart — with one minimal mock-table showing 3 rows of file metadata. That's not a wireframe; it's a feature list with borders.

The Interpretability & Ease of Use criterion is worth 40 points — the biggest single category. Judges scoring this will look for either (a) annotated Figma-style UI mockups or (b) screenshots from the working prototype. Neither is present.

Fix: you have a working prototype. Capture 4-6 screenshots from app/ (Source Intake actually rendering, Harmonized Record with real Synthea data, the assistant answering a question with citations, the Publish Chart flow). Annotate them lightly. This converts the weakest-scoring section into the strongest. ~2-3 hours of work, potentially +8-12 rubric points.

3. "CLI review" is in the cover positioning High

Page 1 cover lists features as "summaries, charts, Q&A, CLI review, and clinician-ready handoffs." A CLI is a developer/auditor tool. For a competition centered on patient-facing usability, advertising "CLI review" on the cover signals "this is built for technical users." It's not what most competitors will lead with.

Fix: remove "CLI review" from the cover. Move it to the "Portable tooling surface" section on page 6 where it belongs (developer/audit path), and reframe it as "developer / audit verification" rather than "CLI review." Consumer-facing language everywhere else.

4. Team section misses interdisciplinary collaborators High

The official rules explicitly call out "interdisciplinary engagement, community engagement, relevant collaborators such as clinicians, patients, UX designers, health IT experts" as things to describe in the team section. The current bio is one paragraph about Blake at Cedars-Sinai — no clinician advisor, no patient advocate, no UX collaborator named. A judge reads this as "solo submission, no clinical validation."

Fix: if you've shown this to ANYONE in a clinical or design capacity — even informal feedback — name them with a one-line role description. Even "informal advisory input from [person], [specialty], Cedars-Sinai" is meaningfully better than nothing. If truly solo, acknowledge it clearly and frame as "Phase 2 will recruit a clinical advisory panel" — that signals self-awareness rather than blind spot.

5. Synthea numbers are framed as a novel achievement High

Page 5 says "approximately 1,180 patients and 527,000+ resources." Synthea is publicly available test data — that number is "the size of the dataset I downloaded," not a prototype achievement. A health-IT judge will notice. The actual achievement is that the application surfaces meaningful clinical information across 1,180 patients — which is real work — but the current phrasing reads as inflated.

Fix: reframe as "Tested across 1,180 synthetic patients (Synthea R4 corpus), surfacing safety panel, interactions, timeline, and care-journey views over 527K resources." Same numbers, different emphasis — the work is in the SURFACES, not in the data load.

6. MedGemma vs Gemma 4 inconsistency Medium

Page 5 says "MedGemma 4B / Ollama pipeline." The codebase uses gemma-4-31b-it in the gemma-tabular pipeline. MedGemma is Google's medical-tuned variant; Gemma 4 31B is the general-purpose vision model. They're different models. If MedGemma 4B was actually installed and tested, that's worth mentioning specifically. If the report meant Gemma 4 31B, the size and label are wrong.

Fix: reconcile. Pick the one that's actually being run, name it correctly, and note its size honestly. Better to say "Gemma 4 31B via Ollama" than risk a judge spotting the inconsistency.

7. Format compliance not verified Medium

Official rules require: PDF (✓), ≤10 pages (✓ at 8), one-inch margins (uncertain), Calibri 11-point or larger (uncertain — appears to be a different sans-serif), no federal logos (✓), no federal endorsement implication (✓). The Calibri requirement is unusual but explicit.

Fix: verify margins are ≥1 inch on all sides. Verify body text is Calibri (or switch to it) at 11pt+. If the current font is similar but not Calibri, a strict reader could disqualify on format grounds — unlikely, but real risk.

8. No demonstration of actual AI output beyond one example Medium

The surgery-clearance example pattern is good, but it's the only concrete AI demonstration in the document. The AI bonus is 20 points and rewards transparent/explainable AI. One example doesn't fully showcase the pattern.

Fix: add one more example pattern — different question, different shape. Suggested: "What changed since my last visit?" with citations to specific encounters and a trend chart. Demonstrates the multi-source reconciliation wedge and the longitudinal use case.

9. Defensive framing repeats Medium

"The model is not the product" / "It is not a PDF chatbot" / "The point is not that one Atlas assistant beats every model" — variations of this defensive framing appear 4-5 times across 8 pages. Once is sharp positioning; repeated, it reads as an author worried about being mistaken for something they're not, rather than confidently selling what they ARE.

Fix: keep the strongest one (page 1 callout box) and remove the others. Use the recovered space for the wireframe screenshots from issue #2.

10. Impact section is generic Medium

Page 8 impact buckets ("Patients understand the record, ask better questions, see trends") are what every competition submission will claim. No quantified outcomes, no specific scenarios, no comparison vs current state. The judge reading 30+ submissions in a row will skim this section.

Fix: at least one quantified or scenario-specific claim per bucket. Even speculative, with disclosure: "Reduces specialist visit prep from ~2 hours of record review to a 5-minute summary review (target; to be measured in Phase 2)." Specific beats generic every time.
