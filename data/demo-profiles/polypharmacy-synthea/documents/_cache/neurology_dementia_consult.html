ASSESSMENT

1. Alzheimer's Disease, Mild-to-Moderate Stage (Primary Diagnosis)

This 92-year-old female presents with progressive cognitive decline over approximately 18-24 months, characterized by significant impairment in short-term memory, executive function, visuospatial abilities, and orientation. The clinical presentation is consistent with Alzheimer's disease based on the following:

  • Insidious onset and gradual progression of cognitive symptoms
  • Prominent episodic memory impairment with poor encoding and recall (0/3 on MMSE delayed recall)
  • Impairment in multiple cognitive domains including executive function, visuospatial skills, and orientation
  • MMSE score of 18/30, consistent with mild-to-moderate dementia
  • Functional decline affecting instrumental activities of daily living, particularly medication management and financial management
  • MRI findings showing generalized cerebral atrophy with medial temporal lobe predominance, supporting neurodegenerative etiology
  • Exclusion of reversible causes (normal thyroid function, normal B12, no acute structural lesions)
  • Positive family history of dementia in mother

The periventricular white matter changes on MRI suggest a component of cerebrovascular disease, which may be contributing to the overall cognitive picture (mixed dementia). However, the clinical presentation and pattern of deficits are most consistent with Alzheimer's disease as the primary pathology.

2. Medication Management Safety Concerns

The patient's cognitive impairment significantly compromises her ability to manage her complex medication regimen safely. This is of particular concern given:

  • Polypharmacy with 12+ current medications
  • Use of high-risk medications including warfarin (narrow therapeutic index, bleeding risk) and insulin (hypoglycemia risk)
  • Documented episodes of medication errors (double-dosing)
  • Impaired executive function and prospective memory necessary for medication adherence

3. Safety Concerns — Risk for Wandering and Getting Lost

The patient has demonstrated spatial disorientation with at least one documented episode of becoming lost in a familiar environment. This represents a significant safety risk.

4. Complex Medical Comorbidities

The patient's multiple medical conditions (atrial fibrillation, diabetes with complications, chronic kidney disease, anemia, ongoing cancer treatment) add complexity to management and may impact cognitive function. Close coordination with her primary care physician and other specialists is essential.
