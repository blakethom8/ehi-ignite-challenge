# Ester635 — Document Sources

Five synthetic documents complement the Synthea FHIR Bundle, simulating the patient's experience of being a 99-year-old polypharmacy patient: a recent discharge summary, her warfarin clinic's INR record, her dementia consult, her colonoscopy and pathology, and her referring PCP's outside C-CDA.

All documents were generated by `scripts/synthesize_documents.py` using the FHIR Bundle as ground truth.

## What's in `documents/`

| File | Source | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `discharge_summary_recent.pdf` | Hospital Internal Medicine | Most recent observation stay (fall + UTI) |
| `anticoag_clinic_letter.pdf` | Outpatient Coumadin (warfarin) Clinic | 6-month INR trail + dose history |
| `neurology_dementia_consult.pdf` | Neurology / Memory Disorders Clinic | Cognitive assessment + galantamine initiation |
| `colonoscopy_pathology.pdf` | GI / Endoscopy + Pathology | Rectal lesion biopsy (invasive adenocarcinoma) |
| `outside_pcp_ccda.xml` | Community primary care provider | C-CDA from referring PCP — STALE record (pre-warfarin) |
| `home_monitoring_fhir_feed.json` | Lively Care home-monitoring platform | 60 days of patient-collected FHIR Observations — connected BP cuff + glucometer + weight |

## Deliberate inconsistencies (the demo wedge)

### 1. Aspirin (outside PCP) vs. Warfarin (in-house)

- The **outside PCP C-CDA** lists **aspirin 81 mg daily** as the patient's antithrombotic.
- The **in-house FHIR record** shows her on **warfarin 5 mg** (managed by the anticoag clinic for her atrial fibrillation).
- The PCP record was sent over when she first established at the new specialist office and predates her starting warfarin — but nobody updated it on the PCP side.
- **Why this matters:** if a downstream provider trusts the outside CCDA, they may co-prescribe aspirin on top of warfarin, materially increasing bleeding risk in a 99-year-old already on FOLFOX (which is itself thrombocytopenic). Atlas should flag the antithrombotic mismatch and the dual-therapy bleeding risk it would create.

### 2. Uncontrolled HTN: home BP data contradicts the chart's qualitative status

- The **home monitoring FHIR feed** shows 60 days of home blood-pressure readings averaging **~170/95 mmHg** (max systolic 179, 9 readings over 170).
- The **in-house chart** lists "Essential (primary) hypertension" as a problem with **no qualitative "uncontrolled" modifier and no recent BP measurement showing the issue**.
- **Why this matters:** the patient is being treated as if her HTN is controlled, but the quantitative home data shows she's clinically uncontrolled. Harmonization should compare quantitative home data against the qualitative chart status and surface the conflict — particularly important here because uncontrolled HTN on warfarin elevates intracranial hemorrhage risk in a 99-year-old.

### 3. Glucose-insulin effectiveness gap

- The **home monitoring FHIR feed** shows blood-glucose readings averaging **~270 mg/dL** (every reading over 250).
- The **in-house chart** has her on Humulin 70/30 insulin, with diabetes coded as a problem but no recent A1c or response monitoring.
- **Why this matters:** the insulin regimen isn't doing the job. Harmonization should detect medication-effectiveness gaps by comparing the prescribed therapy against the actual physiologic response captured in home data — even when both data points individually look fine in their source system.

## How to regenerate

```bash
uv run python scripts/synthesize_documents.py --persona polypharmacy-synthea
uv run python scripts/synthesize_documents.py --persona polypharmacy-synthea --doc outside_pcp_ccda --force
```

Raw HTML used to render each PDF is cached at `documents/_cache/<name>.html`.
