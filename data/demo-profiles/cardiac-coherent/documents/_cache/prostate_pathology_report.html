Detailed Microscopic Findings – Malignant Cores:

Core C (Right mid lateral): This core shows infiltrative prostatic adenocarcinoma characterized by crowded small to medium-sized acini with enlarged nuclei, prominent nucleoli, and loss of basal cell layer. The tumor shows predominantly Gleason pattern 3 (well-formed glands) with a minor component of Gleason pattern 4 (fused glands and cribriform architecture), comprising approximately 20% of the core length. Immunohistochemistry confirms absence of basal cells in neoplastic glands (34betaE12 negative).

Core I (Left mid lateral): Adenocarcinoma is present, exhibiting similar histologic features with Gleason pattern 3 predominance and focal pattern 4 (poorly formed glands with incomplete lumina). Tumor involves approximately 15% of the core. Nuclear atypia is moderate with visible nucleoli.

Core J (Left mid medial): Adenocarcinoma occupies approximately 10% of the core, demonstrating predominantly Gleason pattern 3 architecture with a minor component of pattern 4 (fused and cribriform glands). The neoplastic glands show loss of basal cell layer and nuclear enlargement with prominent nucleoli.

COMMENT:

The findings are diagnostic of prostatic adenocarcinoma with Gleason score 3+4=7 (Grade Group 2, WHO/ISUP 2014 classification). The tumor involves 3 of 12 cores (25% of cores), predominantly in the mid-prostate regions bilaterally with approximate overall tumor burden of 15%. Gleason pattern 4 comprises a minor component in all positive cores.

Grade Group 2 prostatic adenocarcinoma is considered intermediate risk disease. The absence of perineural invasion in the examined tissue is noted, although this finding does not exclude its presence elsewhere in the prostate. Extraprostatic extension cannot be reliably assessed in needle core biopsy specimens.

Clinical correlation with serum PSA levels, digital rectal examination findings, and radiologic imaging is recommended for treatment planning. Consideration for radical prostatectomy, radiation therapy, or active surveillance protocols should be discussed in the context of the patient's overall medical condition, comorbidities, and life expectancy.
