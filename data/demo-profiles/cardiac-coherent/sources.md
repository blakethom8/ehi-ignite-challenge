# Brady998 — Document Sources

Five synthetic documents complement Brady998's Coherent Synthea FHIR Bundle. Brady998 already ships with multimodal raw artefacts (DICOM MRI + DNA file + 1,023 SOAP-style notes embedded in the bundle), so these documents add the *separate-source-of-record* layer: a cardiology consult after his cardiac arrest, his neurology stroke discharge, his urology pathology and oncology plan for prostate cancer, and the human-readable radiology read of the MRI that the platform already has the raw DICOM for.

All documents were generated by `scripts/synthesize_documents.py` using the FHIR Bundle as ground truth.

## What's in `documents/`

| File | Source | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `cardiology_post_arrest_consult.pdf` | Inpatient Cardiology | Workup after cardiac arrest — EF, coronary review, plan |
| `stroke_discharge_summary.pdf` | Stroke / Neurology Service | Acute ischemic stroke discharge — tPA given, residual left arm monoparesis |

| `oncology_chemo_plan.pdf` | Medical Oncology / GU | Treatment plan letter — leuprolide + docetaxel |
| `cardiac_mri_report.pdf` | Cardiothoracic Radiology | Radiology read companion to the raw DICOM in `imaging/` |
| `pacemaker_telemetry_fhir.json` | Medtronic CareLink remote monitoring | 6 months of device-generated FHIR Observations — AT/AF burden, % pacing, lead impedance, battery |

## Multimodal artefacts in sibling directories

- `imaging/Brady998_*.dcm` — the cardiac MRI DICOM. The MRI report PDF is its human-readable companion. The platform does not parse the DICOM — it's a source-of-record artefact.
- `genomics/Brady998_*_dna.csv` — simulated genome variants from the Coherent data set. Again, opaque — included for breadth, not parsed.

## Deliberate inconsistencies (the demo wedge)

### 1. Docetaxel: every 3 weeks (narrative) vs. every 4 weeks (structured)

- The **oncology chemo plan letter** narrates docetaxel "every 3 weeks for 6 cycles."
- The corresponding **MedicationRequest** resources in the FHIR Bundle are coded with a dispensing interval of **every 4 weeks**.
- **Why this matters:** chemo-dose intensity is one of the most safety-critical drug-frequency mismatches in oncology. Either the prescriber said 3 weeks and the order was entered wrong, or the prescriber changed their mind and the letter is stale. Atlas should flag the schedule discrepancy before the next infusion is scheduled.

### 2. AF burden: device telemetry shows worsening, in-house chart only has qualitative "paroxysmal"

- The **pacemaker telemetry FHIR feed** shows AT/AF burden trending up over 6 months — from ~5% in February to ~22% by August. That's substantial paroxysmal-becoming-persistent AF.
- The **in-house chart** codes AF qualitatively as just "paroxysmal atrial fibrillation" with no burden quantification and no progression noted.
- **Why this matters:** harmonization needs to reconcile a quantitative telemetry trend with a static qualitative diagnosis. The numerical truth (worsening AF) should drive a clinical re-think — possibly more aggressive rate control or rhythm strategy — that the qualitative code alone wouldn't surface.

### 3. RV pacing: telemetry shows 38%, in-office interrogation PDF shows 4%

- The **pacemaker telemetry FHIR feed** (most-recent reading) shows RV pacing at ~38%.
- The **pacemaker_interrogation.pdf** (an in-office interrogation roughly 6 months earlier) shows RV pacing at 4%.
- **Why this matters:** these two sources describe the same device at different time points. Harmonization should not silently pick one — it should surface the temporal version conflict, attribute each value to its source/timestamp, and let the clinician see the trend. Particularly important here because >40% RV pacing is associated with pacing-induced cardiomyopathy in a patient who already has reduced EF.

## How to regenerate

```bash
uv run python scripts/synthesize_documents.py --persona cardiac-coherent
uv run python scripts/synthesize_documents.py --persona cardiac-coherent --doc oncology_chemo_plan --force
```

Raw HTML used to render each PDF is cached at `documents/_cache/<name>.html`.
